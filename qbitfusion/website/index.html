One download.
Two ways to finish it.

qBitFusion is a torrent app that can also fetch the same files from your debrid service. It uses the service when it can, turns to the torrent swarm when it can't, and carries on from exactly where it was. Nothing starts over.

New to debrid?

Torrents depend on people.
Debrid services don't.

A torrent downloads from whoever happens to be sharing it. When only a few people are, it crawls, or stalls at 97% for good. A debrid service is a paid service with fast servers that fetch the torrent for you and hand you the files as an ordinary download.

Already use one? Skip to what's new

  1. You hand it a torrent. A magnet link or a .torrent file, the same as always.
  2. Its servers fetch it. Popular files are often there already, because someone else asked first.
  3. You download it like a web file. Over HTTPS, at your connection's speed, and peers never see your IP address. The service does see what you download.

The catch: you usually pick one or the other. A debrid download happens in a separate app, cut off from the torrent, and if the service drops out halfway you start again somewhere else.

How qBitFusion works

Your service and the swarm,
in one download.

qBitFusion treats your debrid service as one more source for the torrent itself. One source works at a time, and when it hands over to the other, everything already downloaded stays put.

Your choice, per download

Decide how each torrent
is allowed to download.

Pick a mode when you add a torrent, and change it later from its menu.

Debrid only, for privacy

Everything comes from your service. Your computer never connects to the torrent swarm, not even to look the torrent up, and it doesn't seed. If the service lacks part of it, the download stops and tells you.

Debrid preferred, for getting it done

Your service supplies everything it has, and the swarm fills in the rest. Here the service is missing one file, so those pieces come from peers. Using the swarm needs your permission for that torrent, because peers can see your IP address. Without it, the download waits for your service instead.

BitTorrent, as usual

A normal torrent download, exactly as in qBittorrent. Your debrid service isn't involved, and the torrent seeds as usual.

Features

The details that make it work.
And keep your files intact.

Every piece is checked.

A torrent carries a fingerprint for every small piece of its files. Pieces from your service are checked against it exactly like pieces from peers, and anything that doesn't match is thrown away and fetched again. Mixing sources can't corrupt your download.

0finished pieces lost

Switching never starts over.

Finished pieces stay finished when the source changes. If your service has an outage, the download doesn't restart.

Takes what your service has.

If your service has only some of a torrent's files, it supplies those, and peers can fill in the rest.

Always says where data comes from.

A Source column shows where each download is coming from and, when it isn't your service, why.

Your login stays locked away.

Your debrid login is kept in the Windows Credential Manager, never in settings files or logs.

Switched off, it's just qBittorrent.

Turn hybrid sources off and it behaves exactly like qBittorrent. It keeps its own settings and runs alongside a normal qBittorrent install without touching it.

Platforms

Built and tested on Windows 11 only (MSVC, Qt 6.10.3, libtorrent 2.1.2). Linux and macOS have never been compiled.

Providers

Real-Debrid only. Torrents that are v2-only can't use it.

Not yet seen live

Switching between the service and the swarm mid-download is covered by automated tests, but hasn't happened yet with a real account. Nor have several selected files in one torrent, a link expiring mid-transfer, OAuth token refresh, and adding a torrent to the account from the app.

Privacy

"Debrid only makes no swarm connections" is enforced in code and tested at the API level, but not yet confirmed with a packet capture. Your provider still knows what you download.

Debrid only

If the provider lacks part of a torrent, the download stops near the end and says so. It doesn't warn you at the start.

Speed

Four parallel HTTPS requests per torrent by default. Whether that holds back fast connections hasn't been measured.

Statistics

"Downloaded" and session totals count only bytes from peers. Progress, speed and ETA are correct.

Web UI and Web API

No way to see or set the download source.

Translations

New text is in English only.

Distribution

A portable zip that updates itself. No installer and no code signing yet, so Windows SmartScreen warns on first start.

Updates

The updater can't yet roll back a version that installs fine and then fails to start. An upstream release that merges cleanly and passes the tests ships without anyone looking, and the tests don't download from a real provider.

Try qBitFusion.

For Windows 10 and 11, 64-bit. It's portable and keeps itself up to date. Settings stay in a folder beside the program, and an existing qBittorrent profile is never read or changed.

Download

Preview builds aren't code-signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen may warn you before the first run.

No preview build has been published yet. Until then, build it from source with Visual Studio 2022, Git, Python 3 and PowerShell 7:

git clone https://github.com/CoderNoel/qBitFusion.git
cd qBitFusion
pwsh qbitfusion/scripts/bootstrap-windows.ps1
pwsh qbitfusion/scripts/install-windows.ps1

qBitFusion is a download client. Use it for content you have the right to download and within your provider's terms. It doesn't work around provider limits, blocks or takedowns.
